    Import from a spreadsheet

    Add new items and update existing ones from a CSV file (in Excel: File → Save As → CSV). Rows are matched to your items by code, or by name when there's no code. Empty cells leave things as they are. Columns: Code, Item, Category, Supplier, Unit, On hand, Low stock level, Cost, Cost currency, Price GBP / USD / EUR / ZAR (or just Price) and Notes. You see every change before anything is saved.

    Your WooCommerce shop

    Bring your shop's products in with their names, codes, categories, prices, stock levels and pictures. In WordPress, go to WooCommerce → Settings → Advanced → REST API → Add key, choose Read permissions, and copy the key and secret here. Invaro only reads from your shop; the key stays on this computer.

    Stock on invoices

    Saving an invoice takes its stock items off what's on hand. Editing it later changes only the difference, and removing it from History puts the items back. Quotes don't take stock until they're made into an invoice.

    Reports

    Totals in rand (ZAR)
    Marking an invoice paid records every bank's rate that day. Standard Bank also has rates for every past date; Absa and Nedbank only publish today's, so for an invoice marked paid on an earlier day without one, Invaro uses Standard Bank's and says so. FNB, Capitec and Investec don't publish rates Invaro can read.

    Totals include VAT/tax. Quotes aren't counted. Rand values use each invoice's own rate, so they stay the same however the rand moves later. Invoices saved before version 1.12.0 have no paid status, so they count as invoiced but not as paid or outstanding until you mark them paid in History.
